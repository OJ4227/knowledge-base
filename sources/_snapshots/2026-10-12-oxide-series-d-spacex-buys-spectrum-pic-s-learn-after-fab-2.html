Headlines and Launches

Oxide Computer Company Secures $445M Series D Funding Round (3 minute read)

Who's building on-prem compute in 2026? It turns out a lot of people! Oxide Computer Company, which builds custom on-prem cloud compute systems, has closed a massive $445 million Series D funding round led by Eclipse, with participation from major investors including Jane Street, Atreides Management, and chipmaker AMD. The capital injection follows the startup achieving operational profitability and will be used to scale manufacturing capacity, secure raw material inventory, and fulfill a rapidly expanding order backlog for their commercial cloud computer racks.

SpaceX buys 800 MHz low-band spectrum to pair terrestrial towers with 2 GHz Starlink satellites (3 minute read)

SpaceX agreed to buy up to 14 MHz of paired 800 MHz low-band spectrum, which penetrates buildings, and plans to deploy its own antennas on towers and rooftops to use it, with most existing phones already supporting the band. Separately, the FCC approved 15,000 more Starlink satellites optimized for 2 GHz. Together they point to a hybrid network, terrestrial indoors and satellite beyond towers. The deal needs FCC approval, and Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile shares fell roughly 6% on the news.

Engineering and Applications

The Machines That Make the Machines: Advancing Industrial Digital Twins (5 minute read)

In this rare peek under the hood, we get a glimpse of how Foxconn uses NVIDIA Omniverse, Isaac, and Metropolis platforms to create physically accurate digital twins of manufacturing facilities that assemble next-generation AI server hardware. By simulating factory layouts, automated optical inspection systems, and autonomous mobile robot fleets in virtual environments before physical deployment, the company minimizes factory commissioning delays and boosts production efficiency. This approach showcases how system architects and automation specialists can leverage high-fidelity simulation to safely validate and optimize complex robotic workcells prior to hardware installation.

Blue Origin rebuilds LC-36 for vertical New Glenn integration, targeting a December return (4 minute read)

After May's pad explosion destroyed its transporter-erector, Blue Origin is rebuilding LC-36 for vertical integration, loading New Glenn through the support tower alone, as with Starship. Crews destacked the tower, added cryogenic propellant plumbing on the ground, and have begun restacking modules, while the launch bridge is restored. Umbilicals and fairing access platforms haven't appeared yet. Bezos targets a December return to flight carrying Blue Ring, though he joked it could slip to December 31.

Research and Prototypes

Photonic chips learn after fabrication by measuring their own optical gradients (3 minute read)

A Nature Computational Science briefing describes an in situ learning method where photonic integrated circuits directly measure optical gradients, so diverse circuit layouts can be trained after they're built. That removes the need for a full digital model of the chip, which optical training normally leans on. The team demonstrated matrix design, multiwavelength computation, and rapid meta-learning on the hardware. Specifics like accuracy or energy figures aren't included.

ScaRF-SLAM: Scale-Consistent Reconstruction with Feed-Forward Models and Classical Visual SLAM (2 minute read)

ScaRF-SLAM is a dense visual mapping system that pairs the reliability of classical visual SLAM with modern feed-forward geometric foundation models for 3D scene reconstruction from the Oxford Robot Dynamics Lab. By decoupling camera tracking from dense depth estimation and optimizing scale consistency across submaps, it achieves globally accurate 3D maps across diverse sensor configurations. For perception engineers and autonomy developers, this framework provides a practical way to produce metric-scale environmental maps in real time without sacrificing tracking stability

Q8botOne: A Palm-Sized Open-Source Quadruped Robot with Smart Actuators (3 minute read)

ZeroWire Robotics has launched the Q8botOne, a smartphone-sized open-source quadruped robot featuring an innovative wire-free chassis where the main printed circuit board serves as the structural frame. Driven by an ESP32-C3 microcontroller and eight DYNAMIXEL smart bus actuators with parallel-linkage legs, the compact robot can walk, trot, jump, and carry twice its 250-gram payload.

Ledger confirms a hardware implant in a tampered wallet as suspected losses near $93M (3 minute read)

Ledger confirmed an unauthorized hardware implant in one affected user's device, bought from Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis, pointing to tampering after manufacturing rather than a breach of Ledger itself. Mark Karpelès published images of a modified Nano X with a small board behind the screen and cellular components, which would presumably capture the 24-word recovery phrase as it displays during setup, leaving the secure element untouched.
